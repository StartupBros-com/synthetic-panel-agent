Synthetic Panel

Which version of your copy lands better? Three synthetic personas each react to two or three versions of a headline, subject line or offer, and their written reactions are scored in your browser with Semantic Similarity Rating.

The ranking compares the versions with each other in one run. It is not a prediction of real conversion or survey results.

How the scoring works, and its limits

Each reaction is embedded with all-MiniLM-L6-v2 and compared with three sets of five anchor statements, one for each point of a 1–5 scale, from "I would definitely pass on this" (1) to "I would be highly inclined to take the next step soon" (5). In each set the similarities become a probability for each point; the three sets are averaged, and the score is the mean of that distribution. This is Semantic Similarity Rating (SSR), ported from synthetic-panel's Python code and checked against its output: on the saved sample run the browser scores match within 0.0034.

The SSR paper (Maier et al., 2025) reports that, on 57 personal-care product surveys with 9,300 human responses, SSR reached 90% of human test-retest reliability with realistic response distributions (KS similarity above 0.85). That was measured with the paper's own anchors, products and models, not with this site's.

synthetic-panel's own limits apply here:

Results are most reliable as relative comparisons among variants tested in the same run, with the same personas, anchors, embedding model, and temperature settings.

v1 reports relative preference signals. Absolute scores are directional.

Synthetic responses inherit the strengths and weaknesses of the AI agent that generated them.

Other agent sites can call the scorer too: score_reactions is shared through this site's action bridge, and it scores the reactions it is sent in a hidden frame in the visitor's browser.
